# R5: sparse LU factorization measurements, dual simplex, benchmark instances

Measurement only. Not the Netlib 93-gate: different instances, 300 s cap per case, one run per case.
Engine: commit 6215801bc05fec1c955849fe1c2c62c50b610be2, built g++ 13.3.0 -O2 -std=c++17, standard library only. The repo working tree and `src/` were not changed.
The counters come from a throwaway instrumented copy of `src/` (diff: instrumentation.patch, sha256 in the hash list; instrumented binary sha256 15843a63...84dd). It counts, per refactorization, basis nnz and L+U nnz (L off-diagonal + U off-diagonal + m pivots), times `SparseLU::factor`, and times the dual's ftran/btran wrappers (LU solves plus eta-file application). The instrumented and plain builds gave identical iteration counts and objectives on ken-11 and pds-10 and no slowdown (wall 10.06 to 10.10 s instrumented vs 10.28 to 10.72 s plain on ken-11; 10.59 to 10.73 s vs 11.00 to 11.32 s on pds-10).
Method: `--method dual --time-limit 300`. Host: Intel Xeon @ 2.80 GHz VM, 4 cores, 16 GB, Linux 6.18.44 (HOST.txt). HiGHS 1.15.1 defaults for references (600 s cap for Kennington/fome from the earlier run, 300 s for the new MIPLIB relaxations; all finished). No random seeds were introduced by this harness; the engine's own dual cost-perturbation seed is a fixed constant in source and was not changed.
Cases: 22 Kennington/Mittelmann (ken-07/11/13/18, osa-07/14/30/60, pds-10/20/30..100, fome11/12/13/21), 8 MIPLIB 3 relaxations (nw04, dano3mip, air04, cap6000, rentacar, mitre, seymour, mod011), 4 MIPLIB 2017 relaxations (app1-2, supportcase10, ex10, s250r10). Relaxation = drop integer MARKER lines and rewrite BV/UI/LI bounds to UP/LO, nothing else (convert_relax.py); HiGHS on the 8 MIPLIB 3 relaxations reproduces the published LP values.

Results
1. 34 cases ran, 0 crashes, 0 singular or failed factorizations. 21 optimal: every one matches HiGHS within 1e-6 relative (worst 7.9e-15) and passes the independent checker at 1e-6 (worst bound violation 6.5e-11; worst row violation 1.49e-8 on m3_rentacar row TOTFIN, which passes the 1e-6 gate but exceeds the 1e-8 strict line). 13 hit the 300 s cap with no answer: ken-18, pds-30..100, fome13, m17_supportcase10, m17_ex10, m17_s250r10. The current implementation times out on these; HiGHS finished all of them (fastest-to-slowest 4.7 s to 185 s).
2. Where it scales: fill-in is small on the network/PDS/OSA/ken structure. Fill ratio (factor nnz / basis nnz) at the last refactorization is 1.0 on ken and osa, 1.0 to 1.025 on pds (max over all refactorizations 1.039, pds-20). Factor storage stays small (largest: supportcase10, about 645k entries). Mean refactorization time grows about linearly in m on pds: 18 ms at m=16,558, 45 ms at 33,874, 100 ms at 66,844, 141 ms at 156,243 (about 1 microsecond per row). One refactorization per about 120 iterations (kRefactorEvery=120) on all cases except those listed below. Peak memory is modest: at most 311 MB (supportcase10), 220 MB on pds-100.
3. Where it hurts, fill: fome11/12/13 ratio 2.2 to 2.4 (fome11: basis nnz 34,860 to factor nnz 76,356; fome13: 133,011 to 240,192), air04 5.3 (5,531 to 29,557), dano3mip up to 3.0 (max over refactorizations). These bases are small to mid-size, so memory is not an issue, but fome refactorizations average 36 to 122 ms.
4. Where it hurts, per-iteration cost: factorization is not the dominant cost. At m >= 40,000 its share of wall is 7% to 27% (pds-40..100: 15% to 21%; pds-30 is the exception in item 5), while ftran/btran take 21% to 74% and the remaining work (pricing, ratio test, updates, parsing, clean-up) 19% to 47%. Wall per iteration rises from 0.14 ms (ken-07, m=2,426) to 8.0 ms (pds-100, m=156,243), 12.6 ms (supportcase10, m=165,684) and 22.5 ms (ex10, m=69,608; solves 74% of wall, basis nnz 259,151, factor nnz 311,229). pds-100 completes 37,376 iterations in 300 s; HiGHS needs 80 s. From reading lu.cpp (not separately measured), ftran/btran loop over all m pivots regardless of right-hand-side sparsity, which matches the near-linear growth of solve time per iteration with m (0.21 ms at m=16.6k to 3.27 ms at m=156k).
5. Anomaly: pds-30 refactored 1,578 times in 69,387 iterations (44 iterations per factor against about 120 elsewhere) and spent 48.8% of its wall in factorization, against 14.6% to 21.3% for pds-40..100. The extra refactorizations come from the dual's stability checks (no leaving row or entering column found on non-fresh factors, or a row/column pivot mismatch, dual.cpp lines 336, 428, 447); why pds-30 triggers them so often was not isolated. s250r10 (99 iterations per factor) and cap6000/mitre/nw04 show smaller deviations.
6. The cap landed inside a factorization on ken-18, pds-30, pds-90 and s250r10 (1 interrupted factorization each); those runs' last complete factor statistics are used. Largest single factorization observed: 632 ms (supportcase10).
7. Not run and why: Mittelmann rail/cre cases (not requested; rail507 is identical to MIPLIB 3 fast0507, which was not run twice); the other MIPLIB 3 models (smaller than the eight chosen) and all other MIPLIB 2017 models (budget: only four were downloaded; larger ones such as dlr1 and square41 are 40 to 80 MB compressed). The default (primal) method was not measured. The dual's final primal clean-up also factorizes; its counts are in the cleanup_* columns and are not mixed into the dual figures. Single runs only; no repeats.
8. Memory: peak RSS is the engine's own VmHWM re-measured by a small launcher for the 17 cases whose timed-run value was 75 MB or less (nine of them showed exactly 32.6 MB, a value inherited from the forking Python parent, so a floor and not a measurement; the re-run's iteration counts were identical to the timed run's); for the other 17 cases it is wait4 ru_maxrss from the timed run (all above 73 MB, so unaffected). The CSV column peak_rss_source says which.
